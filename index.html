HUDA–2026–001 History Begins as Inquiry History’s oldest name describes an activity before it describes a body of knowledge. History Essay EN

The Greek noun historia belongs first to the world of inquiry: looking, asking, comparing reports, and giving an account of what one has learned. That origin does not make Herodotus a modern historian in disguise. It does, however, recover something easily lost when “history” is reduced to a sequence of dates. The discipline begins as a relation between a questioner, witnesses, traces, and a public capable of testing the resulting account.

Herodotus preserves incompatible explanations and marks where knowledge thins into report. Thucydides chooses a sterner economy, scrutinising speeches, causes, and the distortions of memory. Their procedures differ, but both expose the labour between an event and its narration. A source is not a transparent window; it is another historical object, made under pressure and for an audience.

To begin with inquiry is therefore an ethical choice. It asks us to distinguish the date on which an account is published from the period it discusses, to name uncertainty rather than hide it, and to let evidence resist the elegance of our preferred story. An archive becomes historical only when its arrangement can be questioned.

HUDA–2026–002 Der Rand liest mit A German note on scholia, manuscript margins, and the visible history of reading. Literature Scholion DE

Editorial abstract: A margin does not merely surround a text; it records how a text has been handled, taught, disputed, and transmitted.

Eine Randbemerkung steht nicht außerhalb des Textes. Sie zeigt, dass Lesen eine Handlung mit Folgen ist: Ein Wort wird erklärt, eine Stelle angezweifelt, eine Parallele notiert. In Handschriften bildet der freie Raum deshalb kein Schweigen. Er ist die sichtbare Zeit zwischen früheren Sätzen und späteren Lesern.

Die Scholie erinnert außerdem daran, dass Überlieferung selten eine gerade Linie bildet. Abschreiber, Lehrer und Besitzer hinterlassen verschiedene Ordnungen auf derselben Seite. Literaturgeschichte findet nicht nur im Werk statt, sondern auch in seinen Gebrauchsspuren. Wer den Rand entfernt, macht den Text sauberer und seine Geschichte ärmer.

HUDA–2026–003 Logos Is a Relation, Not a Slogan The difficulty of translating logos is a lesson in intellectual caution. Philosophy Note EN

Logos can name speech, account, argument, proportion, or reason. No single English equivalent travels safely through every context. That resistance is useful. It prevents a celebrated word from becoming a decorative emblem and returns it to the sentence, speaker, and dispute in which it does work.

To offer a logos is not merely to possess reason; it is to make something answerable. An account relates a claim to grounds, a part to a whole, and a speaker to listeners who may object. Rationality, on this view, is less a private substance than a public discipline of relation.

Translation should therefore preserve alternatives long enough to ask which one the passage can bear. A glossary is a beginning, not a verdict.

HUDA–2026–005 The Cut Thinks Film does not merely show images; it reasons through the interval between them. Cinema Essay EN

In Battleship Potemkin, meaning does not wait inside an isolated image. It is produced between images: boots descending, faces recoiling, bodies falling, a pram moving through broken space. Eisenstein’s montage compresses and expands time, repeats gestures, and turns collision into argument.

The Odessa Steps sequence is not documentary evidence of a filmed massacre; it is a constructed political scene whose force comes from editing. That distinction makes the film more, not less, useful. It teaches viewers to ask how sequence governs judgment: which shot supplies a cause, which creates an analogy, which converts an individual face into a collective emblem?

Montage exposes cinema as an art of arrangement, yet seamless editing can hide the same power. Every cut distributes attention and responsibility. To watch critically is to notice not only what an image shows, but what the film has placed before and after it.

HUDA–2026–006 Nomos: Before the Rule Becomes a Code Law occupies the unstable passage between inherited practice and articulated rule. Law Essay EN

Nomos can indicate law, custom, convention, or an established distribution. Its range warns against imagining a clean beginning at the moment a rule is written down. Institutions inherit practices before they codify them, and codification changes the practice it claims merely to record.

Written law can make a norm public, portable, and contestable. It can also conceal the judgments required to apply general words to singular lives. Between custom and statute stands interpretation: who may speak, which precedent counts, and what kind of equality the rule presumes.

Legal history should keep that interval open. A code is an event in the life of a norm, not the whole life. Its margins include procedure, enforcement, refusal, and the people for whom the promised universality did not arrive.

HUDA–2026–007 The City, Door by Door W. E. B. Du Bois made Philadelphia’s Seventh Ward legible by joining empirical precision to historical argument. Sociology Field note EN

In 1896, W. E. B. Du Bois began a house-to-house investigation of Philadelphia’s Seventh Ward. He combined interviews with archival research, statistics, observation, institutional records, and maps, then published The Philadelphia Negro in 1899.

The method refused the abstraction of a “social problem” detached from people and place. It documented households, work, housing, education, health, organisations, and the barriers produced by racial discrimination. At the same time, its schedules and categories remind us that social knowledge is never innocent: every survey decides what can be counted and which explanations become visible.

Du Bois’s achievement is not a fantasy of neutral data. It is the more demanding union of empirical precision and historical argument. The city becomes legible neither from a distant theory nor from anecdote alone, but through patient movement between doorstep, archive, table, map, and claim.

HUDA–2026–008 A Catalogue Is an Argument Every index creates neighbourhoods, absences, and paths through knowledge. History Scholion EN

A catalogue appears to stand behind its books, neutral and serviceable. Yet every field is a decision. Names are regularised; disputed dates become sortable; one subject receives a shelf while another survives as a keyword. The index does not only retrieve a collection. It gives the collection a shape.

The lost Pinakes associated with Callimachus at Alexandria matter here less as an intact ancient model—they are not intact—than as a reminder that bibliography has a history. Classification reflects institutions, available evidence, and ideas about which distinctions deserve permanence.

Good metadata should therefore be explicit enough to criticise. HUDA separates publication date from discussed period and keeps sources beside prose so that its own arrangement remains visible.

IV · Editorial note / About HUDA

An archive should show its seams.

Neither museum façade nor infinite feed.

HUDA is an independent humanities publication organised around reading, evidence, and durable reference. Its classical vocabulary names editorial functions rather than claiming an unbroken inheritance: the Stoa holds arguments, the Scholia preserve notes, and the Pinakes make the archive inspectable.

English is the interface language. Contributions may remain in their original German, French, or other language; each record declares its language and may include an English editorial abstract. Translation is treated as scholarship, not a button.
